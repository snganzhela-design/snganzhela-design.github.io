Bone Grafts: Comparative Analysis

Medical review: pending · Updated 26 October 2026 · 5 min read

An overview of common grafting solutions and their clinical properties. The comparison is made between material classes, not individual brands: within each class, products differ in processing, particle size and handling.

For a broader introduction, see Dental Bone Graft types and materials.

Dentin xenograft at a glance

  • Ready to use: supplied sterile, no chairside preparation or second surgical site.
  • No donor-site harvesting: no additional surgery to obtain graft material.
  • Clinical evidence: a randomized controlled trial met its primary endpoint of non-inferiority to a porcine bone xenograft (see Clinical evidence below).

How to read this comparison

Each material class is described by the same criteria:

  • Handling — how the graft is prepared and how easy it is to obtain;
  • Safety profile — harvesting risks, immune response, sterility and the presence of cells;
  • Biological properties — osteoconduction, osteoinduction and what is known about bone formation.

The descriptions summarise the general literature on each class. The choice of graft for a particular patient is a clinical decision of the treating dentist or surgeon.

Dentin xenograft (Ivory Dentin Graft™)

  • Source: dentin of porcine (animal) teeth — a xenogeneic material.
  • Preparation: ready-made graft; dried, uncompacted granules that are hydrated before use.
  • Availability: CE marked (MDR 2017/745), supplied through local distributors; vials and syringes.
  • Harvesting risks: none — no donor site.
  • Immune response: cell-free processed dentin matrix; all biocompatibility testing concluded the material can be regarded as safe. Contraindicated in patients with known or suspected hypersensitivity to porcine products.
  • Sterility: sterilized by gamma irradiation.
  • Cells: no cells in the processed dentin.
  • Biological properties: osteoconductive scaffold; dentin matrix with a retained organic component (type I collagen); osteoinductive potential is reported in the literature for the dentin class; slow resorption in parallel with new bone formation.

Dentin autograft

  • Source: the patient's own extracted teeth.
  • Preparation: chairside processing of the extracted teeth by the clinician with a dedicated grinding device — a technical, time-consuming step.
  • Availability: depends on the teeth extracted from the same patient.
  • Harvesting risks: minor — requires extracted teeth; no separate bone harvesting.
  • Immune response: none expected (own tissue).
  • Sterility: not applicable (own tissue, processed chairside).
  • Cells: no cells in dentin.
  • Biological properties: osteoconductive; osteoinductive potential reported for the dentin class; good bone formation reported in clinical studies.

Bone autograft

  • Source: the patient's own bone, harvested surgically (intraoral or extraoral donor site).
  • Preparation: harvested by the surgeon during a surgical procedure.
  • Availability: limited by the volume of bone that can be harvested.
  • Harvesting risks: medium — a second surgical site with possible pain, morbidity and longer surgery.
  • Immune response: none expected (own tissue).
  • Sterility: not applicable (own tissue).
  • Cells: contains the patient's own living cells.
  • Biological properties: osteogenic, osteoinductive and osteoconductive; traditionally regarded in the literature as the reference material, with faster resorption that may reduce graft volume over time.

Bone allograft

  • Source: processed human donor (cadaver) bone from tissue banks.
  • Preparation: ready-made graft.
  • Availability: commercially available in a wide range of forms.
  • Harvesting risks: none for the patient.
  • Immune response: low; depends on processing.
  • Sterility: sterilized; donor screening and processing reduce the risk of disease transmission.
  • Cells: depends on processing — most processed allografts are cell-free.
  • Biological properties: osteoconductive; osteoinductive potential varies with processing (for example, demineralized forms); bone formation varies by product.

Bone xenograft

  • Source: processed animal bone (most often bovine or porcine).
  • Preparation: ready-made graft.
  • Availability: commercially available in a wide range of forms.
  • Harvesting risks: none for the patient.
  • Immune response: low; depends on processing.
  • Sterility: sterilized.
  • Cells: depends on processing — deproteinized products are cell-free.
  • Biological properties: osteoconductive scaffold with slow resorption; osteoinductive properties are generally not attributed to deproteinized bone.

Synthetic graft

  • Source: synthetic materials — calcium phosphates (hydroxyapatite, β-tricalcium phosphate), bioactive glass and their combinations.
  • Preparation: ready-made graft.
  • Availability: commercially available.
  • Harvesting risks: none.
  • Immune response: none expected for biocompatible materials.
  • Sterility: sterilized.
  • Cells: no cells.
  • Biological properties: osteoconductive; further biological activity depends on composition and additives; bone formation and resorption vary by material.

Clinical evidence for Ivory Dentin Graft

  • Primary endpoint: in a randomized controlled clinical trial, Ivory Dentin Graft was non-inferior to a porcine-derived bone xenograft (PMC10264522).
  • Secondary outcomes (histology, same trial): good graft integration in 85% of sites vs 40% (p = 0.0066); new bone at re-entry 60.75% vs 42.81% (p = 0.0084) — numerically higher than the comparator.
  • Dentin class: a meta-analysis of dentin-graft studies reported +12.4% new bone compared with bone xenografts (p < 0.001, high certainty of evidence) (PMC12149069). This result refers to dentin grafts as a class, not to a specific product.
  • Limitations we state ourselves: the trial was sponsored by the manufacturer, conducted at a single centre and included 36 patients.

Publications and clinical cases are collected in Education.

Choosing a graft

Each material class has its place in bone reconstruction. Indications, contraindications and instructions for Ivory Dentin Graft are given in the instructions for use and certificates. For a clinical question or a sample request, contact the Ivory Graft team.

Functions: Filling defects, serving as a scaffold, and hosting bone-creating cells.

Applications: Dental implants, teeth removal (ridge preservation), sinus lifting, jawbone defects.
